# Phase 6 — Extras

SPEC's line for this phase: lyrics; chord symbols; percussion staves +
drum maps; MIDI device input (ALSA on Linux, WinMM on Windows) with step
entry and real-time recording + quantise; guitar tab; transpose dialog,
respell, find; command palette; anything left from earlier phases'
"deferred" lists; a completeness pass against SPEC.md.

Step A (this section) is the words and the staves that are not five lines
of pitches: lyrics, chord symbols (with playback), percussion staves read
through each instrument's drum map, and guitar/bass tablature linked to a
notation staff — model, file, engraving, playback, entry, MusicXML both
ways, the CLI and the guide. The later steps take MIDI input, the
transpose/find dialogs, the command palette and the completeness pass.

### Decisions

- **A syllable is a mark on its note**, not a separate text counted against
  the notes: an inserted note leaves every syllable where it was, the voice
  grammar carries it unchanged, and MusicXML keeps a lyric on its note too.
- **Chord symbols are a line's, at positions**, apart from its notes: a
  lead sheet's changes fall where the harmony moves. They stand over the
  line's top staff. A file keeps one spelling (`CΔ7` is `Cmaj7`), so a
  diff shows a change of chord, not of typing.
- **Chord playback is the line's own notes** (its dynamics, pedal, mixer
  strip), voice -1 and `FromChord`, optionally on another instrument;
  volume 1 sets a voiced chord a little under one of the line's notes
  (`chordLevel` 0.4: at unity a piano comping five-note chords clipped at
  1.48 against a melody at 0.57).
- **The drum map is the instrument's**, in its `.finst`, so swapping a kit
  re-reads the staff and every score using the kit shows the same map.
  Notes stay stored as the key that plays them; the map gives their
  place, head and articulation, and an articulation mark on a note picks
  the sound written with it (a closed hi-hat with "o" plays the open one),
  so the map works both ways. An instrument with no map uses the General
  MIDI kit's (the GM table gained shortcut letters, no other change).
- **Tablature is a view of the staff's notes**, not a second staff of
  copies: one placement (`Tab.VoicePlaces`, the voice walked from its
  start) is what the galley, every system of the page view, MusicXML and
  editing all see, so a system break does not move the hand. `str=` pins a
  note where the writer wants it; an import keeps only the pins that differ
  from what the tablature would choose.
- **A linked tablature goes out as a TAB staff repeating the notation**
  (MuseScore writes linked staves so, and Sibelius users get a TAB staff),
  and such a pair comes back as one staff with its tablature: checked with
  MuseScore 4.7 reading our export and writing its own back.
- **Fret digits take the main number keys only while the tablature has the
  keyboard** (the selection was picked on its strings): elsewhere 2–9 stay
  intervals, as Sibelius has it.
- **Lyrics go under the notes and dynamics below the lyrics** (they are
  placed first, nearest the staff); on a sung staff step B puts the
  dynamics above instead.

### Decisions

- **libasound is loaded at run time**, like the codecs: the editor builds
  where ALSA's headers are missing (CI has none) and starts where ALSA is,
  saying "MIDI input needs ALSA" where it is not; the event layout is
  declared here and checked against the headers at compile time when they
  are present. ALSA's own stderr messages are routed to silence once the
  library is loaded, so a missing `/dev/snd/seq` is our error, not a log line.
- **A MIDI key is a sound.** Step entry and recording write the pitch the
  key played (what thru just sounded), shown where the staff shows it — a
  clarinet's written pitch, an octave down under an 8va — rather than
  reading keys as written pitch.
- **Chords in step entry are keys held together**, as in Sibelius: the
  first key writes, the others join it while it is held.
- **The click and the count-in live in the score player**, which knows
  where every beat falls: the count-in starts the transport on the exact
  frame its last bar ends, with no second clock to line up. A key is
  mapped to a tick from the player's published position plus the time
  since at the tempo slider's speed (less the latency setting).
- **A take replaces the bars it covers in every voice** of its line's
  staves (Flexi-time's way), written by the MIDI importer's own pipeline.
- **Transposing by key moves the signatures; by interval only when every
  line moves** (and asked). A key past six sharps or flats takes the
  enharmonic interval for the notes too.
- **Cross-staff notes stay in their voice**, stored as a mark: rhythm,
  beams, ties and playback are their staff's; only where they are written
  changes. A score with them is engraved twice so a note can be placed
  where the other staff will be. On the staff they are written on they
  count as its notes: their accidentals are reckoned with its bar, and each
  column is arranged with them (a crossed note a second from that staff's
  own moves beside it, its accidental clear of the others').
- **Brackets come from the instruments' categories** (the library's
  folders), on by default; `brackets: false` turns them off. Hidden empty
  staves are off by default (a score is written for a small ensemble more
  often than an orchestra).
- **MusicXML stems are written for every note** and loosened on reading our
  own files, so another program draws what the page draws without our
  round trip filling with stem marks.
- **Open in instrument editor is the Tracker's editor**, on a scratch song,
  rather than a second copy of it; the notation half of a `.finst` (which
  only the Score reads) is edited in the library dialog itself.

### Kept as limits (with why)

- **SMPTE-timed MIDI files** stay refused: they have no beats to put bars
  on (phase 0).
- **A tuplet across a barline** is closed at it on import: standard
  notation does not cross a barline with a tuplet (Gould; MusicXML and
  Sibelius make the same split).
- **SFZ/SF2 features with no place in a `.finst`** (release triggers,
  modulators and their envelope and LFOs, `<curve>`/`<effect>`, stacked
  layers with no round robin) stay counted in the import's notes: the engine
  plays a layer's one sample with its envelope and filter, and these have
  no part in that model.
- **What MusicXML has no element for** stays in the `.fscore`: a part's
  own page layout (a partwise file has one), smooth tempo ramps (only
  their marks go out), the mixer, effects, lanes, params, bindings, game
  markers, a section's loop and next, swing and humanise.
- **Linux codecs** are the system's libraries unless `CODECS_LINUX` bundles
  a folder: distribution `.so` files tie a tarball to one distribution.
- **PDF text is outlines**: SPEC asks for no font embedding; titles are in
  the document's metadata.
- **Recording into a passage with a repeat** stops where the performance
  returns to a bar it has played: one take cannot be written over a bar
  twice.
